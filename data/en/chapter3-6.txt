Upon my return, moving suddenly from such a splendid house to a filthy one made me feel as if I had entered a dark cave from a sunny mountain top. During my exploration, I had been so distracted that I hadn't even noticed the room decorations or the state of the sliding doors, but as I sensed the inferiority of my own residence, I found myself longing for that so-called 'commonplace.' It seems a businessman is indeed greater than a teacher. Thinking this a bit strange, I consulted my tail, and an oracle came from the tip: 'Quite so, quite so.' Entering the sitting room, I was surprised to find Master Meitei had not yet left; he had stuck his cigarette butts into the brazier like a beehive and was talking away cross-legged. Even Kangetsu-kun had arrived at some point. The master was pillowed on his arm, staring vacantly at the rain leaks on the ceiling. It was the usual gathering of the idlers of peace. 'Kangetsu-kun, the name of the lady who spoke your name in her delirium was a secret then, but surely it's fine to tell us now,' Meitei began teasing. 'Even if I tell you, it's one thing if it only concerned me, but it would cause her trouble.' 'Is it still no good?' 'Besides, I made a promise to the wife of Dr. OO.' 'A promise not to tell anyone?' 'Yes,' Kangetsu-kun said, fiddling with his haori cord as usual. That cord was of a purple color not usually found in shops. 'The color of that cord is a bit Tenpo-esque,' the master said while lying down. He was indifferent to the Kaneda incident. 'Indeed, it's certainly not from the Russo-Japanese War era. It’s a cord that wouldn't look right unless worn with a split haori bearing a hollyhock crest and a lacquered hat. They say when Oda Nobunaga went for his wedding, he tied his hair in a tea-whisk style; that must be the kind of cord he used,' Meitei said, his remarks as long-winded as ever. 'Actually, my grandfather used this during the Choshu expedition,' Kangetsu-kun replied seriously. 'Why don't you just donate it to a museum already? For the lecturer on the mechanics of hanging, the Bachelor of Science Mizushima Kangetsu-kun, to look like a shopworn bannerman is a matter of prestige.' 'I could follow your advice, but there are those who tell me this cord suits me very well...' 'Who would say such a tasteless thing?' the master shouted, rolling over. 'It's not someone you know...' 'It doesn't matter if I know them; who is it?' 'A certain lady.' 'Hahaha! She must be quite an eccentric. Shall I guess? It’s the woman who called your name from the bottom of the Sumida River, isn't it? Why don't you try for another "Buddha-hood" wearing that haori?' Meitei jumped in from the side. 'Hehehe, she's no longer calling from the bottom of the water. She's in a pure world toward the northwest...' 'Doesn't sound too pure to me; that’s one gaudy nose,' Meitei said. 'What?' Kangetsu looked puzzled. 'The "Nose" from across the street barged in here earlier. We were truly surprised, weren't we, Kushami-kun?' 'Mmm,' the master grunted while drinking tea in his reclining position. 'Who do you mean by "Nose"?' 'It's the mother of your beloved eternal lady.' 'Oh?' 'Mrs. Kaneda came to ask about you,' the master explained seriously. I watched to see if Kangetsu would be surprised, happy, or embarrassed, but nothing happened. In his usual quiet tone, he said, 'I suppose it's a request for me to marry her daughter,' and fiddled with his purple cord again. 'Far from it. That mother is the owner of a truly magnificent nose...' Just as Meitei was halfway through his sentence, the master interjected: 'Say, I've been composing a haikai-style poem about that nose.' 'You really are carefree. Is it finished?' 'A bit. The first line is: "At this face, a nose festival."' 'And then?' 'The next is: "At this nose, sacred wine offered."' 'And the next?' 'That’s all I’ve done so far.' 'How amusing,' Kangetsu-kun smirked. 'How about adding "Two holes, faintly visible"?' Meitei came up with one immediately. Then Kangetsu added, 'Deep inside, no hair seen; would that work?' While they were stringing together these nonsensical lines, several voices outside near the fence shouted, 'Imado-ware raccoon dog! Imado-ware raccoon dog!' The master and Meitei were a bit startled and peered through a gap in the fence. A roar of laughter—'Wahahaha!'—erupted, followed by the sound of feet scattering into the distance. 'What's an "Imado-ware raccoon dog"?' Meitei asked the master curiously. 'I have no idea.' 'Quite a striking phrase,' Kangetsu-kun remarked. Remembering something, Meitei suddenly stood up and said, 'For years, I have researched this nose from an aesthetic standpoint, and I would like to reveal a portion of my findings for your kind attention,' and began mimicking a lecture. The master, taken aback by the suddenness, stared at Meitei in silence. Kangetsu whispered, 'I should very much like to hear it.' 'I have investigated various sources, but the origin of the nose is not quite certain. My first doubt is that, if we assume it is a practical tool, two holes are quite enough. There is no need for it to protrude so arrogantly from the center. And yet, why has it gradually come to thrust itself forward as you see?' He pinched his own nose to demonstrate. 'Yours doesn't thrust forward much at all,' the master said without flattery. 'At any rate, it isn't sunken. I must warn you beforehand not to confuse it with a state where only two holes are aligned, as that may lead to misunderstanding. According to my humble opinion, the development of the nose is the result of the accumulation of the minute act of human nose-blowing, which has presented itself as this remarkable phenomenon.' 'An honest, humble opinion,' the master interjected. 'As you know, when one blows one's nose, one must pinch it. By pinching and giving stimulation to this specific area, according to the great principles of evolution, this part develops disproportionately compared to others to respond to that stimulus. The skin naturally hardens; the flesh gradually becomes firm. Eventually, it hardens into bone.' 'That's a bit—flesh surely cannot change into bone in a single leap,' Kangetsu-kun protested as a man of science. Meitei continued unperturbed. 'Your doubt is reasonable, but proof is better than argument; the bone is right here, so there's no helping it. Once the bone is formed, the mucus still comes out. If it comes out, one must blow it. By this action, the sides of the bone are scraped away, and it transforms into a thin, high ridge—truly a terrifying process. Like dripping water wearing away a stone, or like the head of Pindola radiating light, or like the metaphor of mysterious fragrance and mysterious scent, the bridge of the nose becomes straight and hard.' 'Even then, yours is quite squishy.' 'I will refrain from discussing the speaker's own features for fear of bias. I wish to introduce the nose possessed by that Kaneda mother to you both as the most developed, most magnificent, and rarest specimen in the world.' Kangetsu-kun let out a 'Hiyayaya!' 'However, when a thing reaches such an extreme, it is indeed a grand sight but somehow terrifying and difficult to approach. That nasal bridge is undoubtedly magnificent, but it seems perhaps a bit too precipitous. Among the ancients, the noses of Socrates, Goldsmith, or Thackeray might have had their flaws in structure, but those flaws were precisely where their charm lay. A nose is not noble because it is high, but because it is unique—perhaps that is the reason. As the proverb says, "Dango over a high nose," in terms of aesthetic value, I believe something like mine is just right.' Kangetsu and the master burst into laughter. Meitei himself laughed with delight. 'Now, what I have just discussed—' 'Sensei, saying "discussed" sounds a bit like a storyteller and is vulgar, so please refrain,' Kangetsu-kun took his revenge. 'Very well, then shall I wash my face and start over? —Ahem— Now I should like to remark briefly on the balance between the nose and the face. If one considers the nose in isolation, that mother possesses a nose that would not be ashamed anywhere—a nose that would likely win first prize at an exhibition on Mount Kurama; but alas, it is a nose that was completed without any consultation with the eyes, mouth, or other such gentlemen. Julius Caesar's nose was undoubtedly a great thing. But what if one were to snip off Caesar's nose with a pair of scissors and place it on the face of the cat in this house? To have the nasal bridge of a hero rise up abruptly on a piece of land like a "cat's forehead" would be like placing the Great Buddha of Nara on a go board; it would lose all proportion and drop in aesthetic value. The mother's nose, like Caesar's, is indeed a sharp and gallant protrusion. But what of the facial conditions surrounding it? Of course, it is not inferior like the cat in this house. But it is a fact that she has slanted eyes and a scowl at the bridge of her brows like a "faced-mask" suffering from a seizure. My friends, one cannot help but lament: this nose on this face!' Just as Meitei paused, a voice from the back called out, 'They're still talking about the nose! What an obstinate bunch!' 'It's the rickshaw man's wife,' the master told Meitei. Meitei began again. 'To discover that there is a new listener of the opposite sex in the unexpected rear is a great honor for the speaker. Especially that a charming voice has added a touch of elegance to this dry lecture is truly a stroke of luck beyond expectation. I intend to simplify my talk for a general audience so as not to disappoint the fair ladies, but since I am about to enter into matters of mechanics, it may be difficult for the ladies to follow; I ask for your patience.' Kangetsu-kun smirked again at the word 'mechanics.' 'What I am trying to prove is that this nose and this face simply do not harmonize. It violates Zeising's Golden Section, and I intend to demonstrate this by strictly deducing it from mechanical formulas. First, let H be the height of the nose. alpha is the angle produced by the intersection of the nose and the plane of the face. W, of course, is the weight of the nose. Do you follow me so far? ...' 'How could we?' the master said. 'How about you, Kangetsu-kun?' 'It's a bit beyond me too.' 'That's a problem. Kushami aside, I thought you'd understand since you're a Bachelor of Science. Since this formula is the heart of the lecture, if I skip it, all my work will be for nothing—but there's no helping it. I'll skip the formula and just give the conclusion.' 'Is there a conclusion?' the master asked curiously. 'Naturally. A lecture without a conclusion is like a Western meal without dessert. —Now, listen well, you two; here is the conclusion. —Looking at the above formulas while considering the theories of Virchow and Weismann, one must of course allow for the inheritance of innate forms. Furthermore, the mental state that follows this form must be recognized as an inevitable result to some extent, despite the influential theory that acquired traits are not inherited. Therefore, the child born of a possessor of such an inappropriate nose is expected to have some abnormality in her nose as well. A young man like Kangetsu-kun might not yet notice any special abnormality in the structure of Miss Kaneda's nose, but such inheritance can have a long latent period; with a sudden change in climate, it may develop abruptly and expand in an instant to become like her mother's. Therefore, based on Meitei's scientific demonstration, I believe it would be safer to abandon this marriage proposal now. I imagine the master of this house, and even the cat-monster lying there, would have no objection to this.' The master finally sat up and asserted with great enthusiasm, 'Of course. Who would marry the daughter of such a person? Kangetsu-kun, you mustn't marry her.' To show my agreement, I let out two 'meow's. Kangetsu-kun, seemingly unperturbed, said, 'If that is your view, Sensei, I don't mind giving it up, but it would be a sin if she took it to heart and fell ill...' 'Hahaha! A "romantic sin," then!' Only the master was truly worked up: 'What nonsense! If she's that person's daughter, she’s bound to be no good. She’s the one who came to a stranger's house and tried to put me down. An arrogant woman!' he fumed. Then, several voices near the fence shouted, 'Wahahaha!' One said, 'A conceited blockhead!' another said, 'He probably wants to move to a larger house!' and another shouted in a loud voice, 'A pity, but no matter how much he boasts, he's just a "hero in the shadows"!' The master stepped out onto the veranda and shouted back with a voice to match, 'Quiet! Why do you come under my wall on purpose?' 'Wahahaha! Savage tea! Savage tea!' they taunted in unison. The master, in a towering rage, suddenly stood up, grabbed his stick, and dashed out into the street. Meitei clapped his hands and shouted, 'Splendid! Go to it!' Kangetsu smirked and twisted his haori cord. I followed the master and slipped through a gap in the fence onto the street, only to find him standing in the middle of it, looking awkward with his stick in hand. Not a single person was passing by; it was as if he had been bewitched by a fox.
