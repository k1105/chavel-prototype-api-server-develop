After such a failure, it was somewhat awkward to stay indoors and be seen by O-san. I decided to change my mood by visiting Mike-ko, who lives at the home of the two-stringed lute teacher in Shimmichi. Mike-ko is a renowned beauty in these parts. I am a cat, but I understand the gentler emotions. When I am out of sorts from seeing the master’s sour face or receiving a sharp rebuke from O-san, I always visit this female friend to talk. Then, before I know it, my heart clears, and I forget all my worries and troubles, feeling as if I have been reborn. The influence of the opposite sex is truly immense.

Peering through a gap in the cedar hedge to see if she was there, I saw Mike-ko sitting elegantly on the veranda, wearing a new collar for the New Year. The roundness of her back was indescribably beautiful, the very essence of curved beauty. The curl of her tail, the way she tucked her paws, and the listless flicking of her ears are beyond description. Especially as she sat gracefully in the warm sun, despite her quiet and refined posture, her fur—smooth enough to put velvet to shame—reflected the spring light, seeming to ripple faintly as if stirred by a phantom breeze. I gazed in a trance for a while, but eventually coming to my senses, I beckoned with my front paw, calling softly, "Mike-ko-san, Mike-ko-san." Mike-ko stepped down from the veranda, saying, "Oh, Sensei!" The bell on her red collar went *tinkle-tinkle*. "Ah, she even has a bell for the New Year. What a lovely sound," I thought in admiration. She came to my side and wagged her tail to the left, saying, "Oh, Sensei, Happy New Year." Among us cats, when we greet each other, we hold our tails upright like a rod and rotate them to the left. Mike-ko is the only one in the neighborhood who calls me "Sensei." As I mentioned before, I have no name, but because I live in a teacher's house, Mike-ko alone respects me enough to call me "Sensei." Since I don't feel entirely bad being called "Sensei," I respond with a polite "Yes, yes."

"Well, Happy New Year. You are very finely dressed up, aren't you?" I said. "Yes, my teacher bought this for me at the end of last year. Isn't it nice?" she replied, jingling the bell. "It is indeed a fine sound. I have never seen such a splendid thing in my life," I admitted. "Oh, don't be silly. Everyone wears them," she said, jingling it again. "Isn't it a lovely sound? I'm so happy," she continued, tinkling it repeatedly. "It seems your teacher is very fond of you," I remarked, secretly feeling a pang of envy compared to my own situation. Mike-ko is an innocent soul. "Truly, she treats me just like her own child," she laughed innocently. Even cats are not incapable of laughing. Humans are mistaken to think they are the only ones who can laugh. I laugh by making my nostrils triangular and vibrating my Adam's apple, so humans naturally cannot tell. "By the way, what is your owner's background?" I asked. "Owner? How strange you are. She's my teacher. A teacher of the two-stringed lute." "I know that much. I mean, what is her social status? She must have been someone of high rank in the past." "Yes," she replied.

*Waiting for you, the Princess Pine...*

From behind the shoji, the teacher began to play the lute. "Isn't that a lovely voice?" Mike-ko boasted. "It seems pleasant, but I don't quite understand it. What is it exactly?" "That? That's... something-or-other. My teacher loves it. She's sixty-two, you know. Quite robust, isn't she?" Being alive at sixty-two, one must call her robust. I replied with a simple "I see." It sounded a bit flat, but since no clever response came to mind, it couldn't be helped. "She says she used to be of very high standing. She's always saying so." "Oh? And what was she, originally?" "Well, she's the daughter of the nephew of the mother of the place where the younger sister of the secretary to Lady Tenshoin went as a bride." "What was that?" "I said, she's from the place where the younger sister of the secretary to Lady Tenshoin went as a bride..." "I see. Wait a moment. The secretary to the younger sister of Lady Tenshoin..." "No, that's not it. The younger sister of the secretary to Lady Tenshoin..." "All right, I've got it. She belongs to Lady Tenshoin?" "Yes." "The secretary?" "That's right." "Went as a bride." "The younger sister went as a bride." "Yes, yes, I got it wrong. The place where the younger sister went as a bride..." "The daughter of the nephew of the mother of that place." "The daughter of the nephew of the mother?" "Yes. You understand now, don't you?" "No. It's all jumbled and I can't make head or tail of it. In short, what is she to Lady Tenshoin?" "You really are slow. As I've been saying, she's the daughter of the nephew of the mother of the place where the younger sister of the secretary to Lady Tenshoin went as a bride!" "I understand all that..." "Then that should be enough, shouldn't it?" "Yes," I said, surrendering as there was no other way. Sometimes we must tell logically sound lies.

When the sound of the lute stopped abruptly behind the shoji, the teacher's voice called, "Mike, Mike, it's time for dinner!" Mike-ko said happily, "Oh, the teacher is calling, so I'll go back now. Is that all right?" It didn't matter if it was all right or not. "Then come and play again," she said, jingling her bell and running to the garden, but then she suddenly turned back. "You look quite pale. Is something wrong?" she asked with concern. I couldn't very well tell her I had been dancing because I ate ozoni, so I said, "It's nothing in particular, but I have a bit of a headache from thinking too much. I thought talking to you might cure it, so I came out." "I see. Do take care of yourself. Goodbye." She seemed a little reluctant to part. My strength from the ozoni incident was now fully recovered. I felt good.

On my way back, intending to pass through that tea garden, I stepped over the melting frost and poked my head through the gap in the Kenninji fence. There was Kuro of the rickshaw shop again, his back arched like a mountain on the withered chrysanthemums, yawning. These days I am no longer terrified of Kuro, but since talking to him is a nuisance, I tried to pass by as if I hadn't noticed him. It is in Kuro's nature never to remain silent if he perceives someone is slighting him. "Hey, Nameless Gonbe! You're putting on quite some airs lately, aren't you? No matter how much of a teacher's rice you eat, don't go making such an arrogant face. It's not the least bit amusing." It seems Kuro doesn't know I've become famous yet. I wanted to explain, but since he's not the type to understand, I decided the best thing was to give a formal greeting and excuse myself as quickly as possible. "Well, Kuro-kun, Happy New Year. You look as energetic as ever," I said, raising my tail and rotating it to the left. Kuro kept his tail raised but didn't return the greeting. "Happy New Year? If being New Year's makes you happy, a fellow like you must be happy all year round. Watch out, you 'bellows-facing' fool!" The phrase "bellows-facing" seemed to be an insult, but I didn't understand it. "May I ask what 'bellows-facing' means?" "Hmph! You're being insulted and you ask for the meaning? That's why you're a 'New Year fool'!" "New Year fool" sounded poetic, but its meaning was even more obscure than the bellows thing. I wanted to ask for reference, but knowing I wouldn't get a clear answer, I just stood there silently facing him. It was a bit of an awkward situation.

Suddenly, the woman at Kuro's house yelled at the top of her lungs, "Oh! The salmon I left on the shelf is gone! This is terrible! That beast Kuro took it again. He really is a hateful cat. Just you wait and see what happens when he comes back!" Her voice rudely vibrated through the peaceful early spring air, utterly ruining the tranquil atmosphere. Kuro put on a defiant face, as if to say she could scream as much as she liked, and jutted out his square chin, signaling me: "Did you hear that?" I hadn't noticed in our exchange, but there under his paw lay a mud-covered bone of salmon, worth about two sen and three rin. "You're at it as usual, I see," I remarked, forgetting our previous tension and offering a word of admiration. Kuro wasn't about to be appeased so easily. "What do you mean 'at it as usual,' you bastard! Calling it 'as usual' over a slice or two of salmon—don't look down on me! I'm Kuro of the rickshaw shop, for your information!" Instead of rolling up his sleeves, he scratched his right front paw up to his shoulder. "I knew you were Kuro-kun from the start." "If you knew, then why say 'at it as usual'? What do you mean by that, eh?" he said, puffing out hot breath. If we were humans, I would have been grabbed by the collar and shoved around. Feeling a bit intimidated and thinking I was in a fix, I heard the woman's loud voice again. "Hey, Nishikawa-san! Oh, Nishikawa-san, I say! I have an order! Bring me a pound of beef right away. You hear? A pound of beef, and make sure it’s tender!" The order for beef broke the surrounding silence. "Hmph! Thinks she can shout like that just because she orders beef once a year. She's so proud of a pound of beef she has to brag to the whole neighborhood. What a hopeless woman," Kuro mocked, bracing his four legs. Having no way to respond, I just watched in silence. "A pound isn't quite enough for me, but it can't be helped. Let her take it; I'll eat it soon enough," he said, as if the order had been made for him. "A real feast this time. Excellent, excellent," I said, trying to get him to leave. "None of your business! Shut up! You're annoying!" he barked, and suddenly, with his hind legs, he kicked a pile of melting frost right onto my head. While I was startled and brushing the mud off my body, Kuro slipped through the fence and vanished. He had likely gone to keep an eye out for Nishikawa's beef.
